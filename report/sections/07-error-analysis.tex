\section{Error analysis and discussion}
\label{sec:errors}

\subsection{Against the literature}

The post-correction literature converges on one warning. Kanerva et al.\ report
that an LLM corrector degrades already-good OCR~\cite{kanerva2025nofreelunches};
the HIPE-OCRepair competition names over-correction on low-noise input as its
consistent problem, and adds that character-level metrics do not capture
it~\cite{ehrmann2026hipeocrepair}. Both describe a corrector that rewrites when
it should abstain.

The marker anchor turns that from a quality problem into a detectable one. The
\corrRewritesCaught{} refusals are replies that changed text outside the
markers, which under a rule without the anchor would have been accepted
silently. But the branch fires on any byte difference outside the markers, so
it bounds fluent rewriting from above: a moved space and a rewritten clause
land in the same bucket. \corrRewritesSubstantial{} of the
\corrRewritesCaught{} moved more than two characters, which is the figure that
carries the claim.

Two limits follow. The refusal rate says the rule works as specified, not that
the text improved; that needs E1's gold set. And HIPE-OCRepair's point about
character-level metrics cuts here too --- a rule that refuses is measured by
what it refuses, which is what this report measures and no more.

\subsection{A hole in the accept rule}

The rule is not as airtight as section~\ref{sec:system} describes.

\texttt{suspect\_spans} locates each suspect word by character offset when the
recogniser's raw text and the page's text align, and otherwise searches for the
word. The search takes the \emph{first} occurrence, so two low-confidence
occurrences of the same word resolve to the same index. The line is then marked
with two markers around one word, the model is shown a corrupted line, and the
accept rule compares the reply against that same corrupted construction --- and
accepts it. The second genuine suspect word is never offered.

The rule cannot catch this, because the corruption happens before it runs. And
the branch is not an edge case: \corrOnFindFallback{} of the \corrOffered{}
offered lines took it, \corrFindFallbackShare{} of the sample. On those lines
an acceptance is not evidence that the rule held, because the rule was
comparing against a line the pipeline had already corrupted.

This is the most serious limitation in the report. The measured acceptance rate
is an upper bound on the rule's clean behaviour, and C2 is supported on under a
third of the lines E2 offered.

One caveat on the number. A failed offset check is necessary for the bug, not
sufficient --- it also needs two low-confidence occurrences of one word on one
line. \corrFindFallbackShare{} is therefore an upper bound on the exposure, not
an estimate of the damage, and measuring the difference would need the spans
themselves, which are text this repository does not hold.
\file{docs/notes/2026-09-22-accept-rule-find-fallback.md} reproduces the case
against the module and gives the fix, which belongs in the pipeline: search
forward from the previous span's end, and refuse to mark a line whose spans
overlap.

\subsection{Localisation: the right book, the wrong chapter}

Livre accuracy is \locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} and chapter accuracy
\locChapterAccuracyWorkbook{} on the same pieces. The pre-registration
anticipated this case and said what it means --- \qtr{Correct CHAPTER far below
correct LIVRE points at the span, not the search} --- and said in the same
breath to tune \texttt{CHAIN\_GAP\_PENALTY} and \texttt{WINDOW\_SLACK} before
concluding it. That tuning has not been done. The span reading is therefore the
pre-registered expectation confirmed in direction, not a tested cause, and this
report does not present it as one.

The denominators say something the rates do not. Only
\locChapterScoreableWorkbook{} of \locPiecesWorkbook{} certain pieces carry a
decodable printed chapter number, so chapter accuracy is measured on a minority
of the cohort and is the weaker of the two figures in evidence as well as in
value.

\subsection{Structure: after hand correction, the residue is spurious openings}

On family~A, \structSpuriousA{} proposed chapter openings remain against
\structMissingA{} misses --- after a reader made \textbf{128 promotions and 217
demotions} by hand. That residue is over-proposal.

It is not evidence about the unaided detector.
\file{docs/notes/2026-09-22-structural-figures-are-hand-checked.md} bounds the
unaided recall as low as 77.0\% against the \structRecallHandCheckedA{}
reported, because the hand pass removed an unknown share of the misses. What
the residue supports is a statement about the corrected detector and nothing
wider.

\subsection{What this section cannot say}

The confusion table the project has asserted for a year --- long~\qtr{ſ}
against \qtr{f}, \qtr{u} against \qtr{n}, \qtr{c} against \qtr{e},
\qtr{H} against \qtr{R} --- is not here: quantifying a substitution needs a
reference transcription. Neither is any comparison of a measured CER to the
literature. Both wait on E1. The code that would compute them
(\file{src/amadis_htr/cer.py}, with its \texttt{substitution\_counts}) is
written and tested against synthetic input.
