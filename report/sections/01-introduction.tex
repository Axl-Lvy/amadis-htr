\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

For the past year I have been building a pipeline for two related works of
sixteenth-century French print. \emph{Amadis de Gaule} is a chivalric romance
first written in Spanish by Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo. Its French version
began in 1540 with the translation by Nicolas Herberay des Essarts and was very
popular from the start. Translators in Paris and then Lyon kept adding new
books, and by 1615 the French cycle had \ocrBooks{} of
them~\cite{debuzon2011chappuys}. The \emph{Trésor des Amadis}, first printed in
1559, collects the speeches, letters, complaints and challenges of the romance
that were judged the most eloquent. It was meant as a model for readers who
wanted to learn to write letters and speak well, and it was reprinted and
enlarged until 1606~\cite{eman2026tresor}. The aim of the pipeline is an
accurate transcription of the cycle, and a way to find where each
\emph{Trésor} extract comes from in it.

This print is hard for standard OCR. The long \qtr{ſ} is a separate character
that modern models read as \qtr{f}. The early volumes are set in
\emph{bâtarde}, a typeface with little modern training data. Chapters open with
drop caps several lines tall, which segmenters take for pictures or noise.
Running heads, catchwords and signatures sit inside the text block. Folio
numbers are roman in half the cycle and arabic in the other half, and the scans
come from several libraries at different levels of quality, one with a stamp
across every page. Figure~\ref{fig:plate} shows a typical page.

The pipeline has three stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Recognition.} A kraken model, fine-tuned from CATMuS-Print on
    pages of the \emph{Trésor}, reads each page.
  \item \textbf{Correction.} The words the model was unsure of are marked, and a
    local language model is asked to correct them. A correction is kept only if
    the rest of the line comes back unchanged.
  \item \textbf{Passage location.} Each \emph{Trésor} extract is searched for in
    the transcribed cycle, and the locator returns the passage it matches and
    the Livre it belongs to.
\end{enumerate}

This report measures each stage in turn. For recognition, I report the validation
curve of the fine-tune and compare two training runs
(section~\ref{sec:training}); no separate test set was scored. For correction,
I report what the accept rule keeps and what it refuses on a sample of
\corrPages{} pages (section~\ref{sec:e2}). For passage location, I report how
often the locator finds the right Livre, checked against the editor's own
catalogue (section~\ref{sec:e5}). Section~\ref{sec:e6} adds
throughput and chapter detection. The correction and location experiments were
designed in writing before they were run.
