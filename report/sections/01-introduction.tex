\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A digitisation pipeline has been reading sixteenth-century French romance for
this project for a year. It has one recognition figure to its name: a
validation character error rate, measured on a 10\% page-level split of a
single volume, on the material the model was fine-tuned on. That number
selected a checkpoint. It cannot tell anyone how the system reads the corpus it
was built for. This report is the measurement.

Sixteenth-century print defeats off-the-shelf OCR for reasons that compound.
The long \qtr{ſ} is a distinct character that modern models map onto \qtr{f}.
The earlier volumes are set in \emph{bâtarde}, a type family with little modern
training data. Chapters open with drop caps spanning several lines, which
segmenters read as illustrations or as noise. Running heads, catchwords and
signatures sit in the text block, folio numbering runs roman in one half of the
corpus and arabic in the other, and the scans come from three institutions at
three qualities, one of them stamped across the page.
Figure~\ref{fig:plate} is one such page.

What the project could state before this report: nothing. No recognition figure
of any kind for \emph{Amadis de Gaule}, the work the system exists to read; no
word error rate anywhere; no measured baseline; no evaluation of either LLM
pass, including the accept rule that is the pipeline's most distinctive
component; and structural figures computed on every run and discarded.

\subsection{Three claims}

\claim{C1.}{The fine-tune reads this corpus better than the published models
do.} Evaluated on annotated pages of each work, one error rate per work,
against published models run under held-constant segmentation.

\claim{C2.}{A conservative accept rule keeps a generative pass from rewriting
the text.} Words carrying a low-confidence character are wrapped in
\lmark~\rmark{} and re-read; the reply is accepted only when everything outside
the markers returns byte identical. The failure mode being defended against is
the one a generative model actually has on this material --- fluent
modernisation of archaic spelling. Section~\ref{sec:e2} reports what the rule
did.

\claim{C3.}{A \emph{Trésor} extract can be located in the baseline text.}
A retrieval task over the full transcription of all \ocrBooks{} books, scored
against the editor's own catalogue under a gate written before the numbers were
known. Section~\ref{sec:e5} reports \locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} Livre accuracy
on the pre-registered cohort.

\medskip\noindent
Of the five experiments that test a claim, three are unmeasured. They are named
as unmeasured, and section~\ref{sec:limitations} says what that costs.
