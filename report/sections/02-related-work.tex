\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{CATMuS and the engine.} CATMuS is a shared transcription standard
before it is a model: its medieval release fixed the annotation guidelines and
the cross-century protocol that make one fine-tune comparable to another at
all~\cite{clerice2024catmusmedieval}, and CATMuS-Print carries the approach
into printed material~\cite{gabay2024catmusprint}. CATMuS-Print~[Large] is the
checkpoint this work fine-tunes from~\cite{gabay2024catmusprintlarge}.
Recognition runs on kraken~7.0.2; the engine paper describes
version~5~\cite{kiessling2025kraken} and the Zenodo record pins
3.0b20~\cite{kiessling2021krakensoftware}, so 7.0.2 is identified by number.
kraken ships no reference model for early-modern French print, which is why
E1's comparator must be a chosen published model rather than a default.
Transkribus supplied the annotation environment~\cite{kahle2017transkribus,
muehlberger2019transkribus}; its models are opaque and it segments
independently, so it enters this evaluation as a confounded baseline.

\textbf{LLM post-correction, and its characteristic failure.} The literature
converges on one warning. Kanerva et al.\ title it directly --- there are no
free lunches: an LLM corrector can degrade already-good OCR rather than improve
it~\cite{kanerva2025nofreelunches}. Boros et al.\ find the same across
prompting regimes on historical transcripts~\cite{boros2024postcorrection}, and
the HIPE-OCRepair competition reports over-correction on low-noise inputs as
its consistent problem, adding that character-level metrics alone do not
capture it~\cite{ehrmann2026hipeocrepair}. A modular ByT5 stage behind kraken
reports a CER reduction on fifteenth-century
incunabula~\cite{momtaz2025modular}, read at its abstract only; Thomas et al.\
reach the opposite design point on historical
newspapers~\cite{thomas2024leveraging}. A model asked to fix a misread \qtr{ſ}
will, given the opportunity, also modernise the spelling around it. The marker
anchor removes the opportunity.

\textbf{The gap.} To our knowledge no character error rate has been published
for a CATMuS-Print fine-tune on a sixteenth-century French romance corpus. The
nearest published work is CATMuS Gothic Print, a different base model on
religious and polemical material, reporting no error
rate~\cite{solfrini2024catmusgothic}, and Tiger~\cite{tiger2026overfit}, which
evaluates HTR fine-tuning on French of the seventeenth and eighteenth
centuries. The claim is held to \emph{to our knowledge} and no further: a
literature search cannot rule out an unindexed thesis or a non-anglophone
venue. Confidence intervals throughout follow the percentile
bootstrap~\cite{efron1994bootstrap}.
