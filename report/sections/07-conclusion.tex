\section{Conclusion and released artefacts}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Recognition.} The fine-tuned model reaches \trainCer{} CER on its
validation pages, down from \trainCerFirstEpoch{} after its first epoch and
below the \trainCerConstant{} of the first training run. It is the only
recognition figure in this report, and it comes from validation pages.

\textbf{Correction.} The accept rule does what it was built to do: it refused
\corrRewritesCaught{} replies that changed text outside the marked words. Whether the kept corrections improve the text is
still an open question.

\textbf{Passage location.} This works. The locator finds the right Livre for
\locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} and \locLivreAccuracyCatalogue{} of the extracts in
the first and second batch, well above the 90\% set in advance. The chapter
numbers read from the transcribed headings agree with the editor's less often,
at \locChapterAccuracyWorkbook{} and \locChapterAccuracyCatalogue{}, and nearly
all the disagreements look like misread roman numerals in those headings.

\subsection{Released artefacts}
\label{sec:artefacts}

The repository contains the scoring code, the frozen run records and every
result CSV, so each figure can be rebuilt without a GPU, a model or a network.
This includes the fine-tune's training logs, the run records of the
\ocrBooks{} books, the correction sample with its verdicts, and the locator's
output. Appendix~\ref{app:data} says where each one is. Because the shipped
model is tied to its training checkpoint, its Hugging Face model card can now
be written.

No page images are released apart from the illustrations in
section~\ref{sec:corpus}, because the reuse terms differ between libraries and
are unclear for at least one. The data are released under CC-BY-4.0. The
transcriptions are new editorial work, and the printed books themselves are
long out of copyright.
