\subsection{Recognition: the fine-tune}
\label{sec:training}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/figures/training-curve.pdf}
  \caption{Validation CER after each epoch, for both training runs. The dot
    marks the shipped checkpoint.}
  \label{fig:training}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:training} shows the validation CER after each epoch for both
runs. The first run, at kraken's default constant learning rate, levelled off
after a few epochs, and its best checkpoint reached \trainCerConstant{}. The
second run stood at \trainCerFirstEpoch{} after its first epoch and kept
improving for much longer. Its best checkpoint, at epoch \trainBestEpoch{} of
\trainEpochs{}, reached \trainCer{} CER and \trainWer{} word error rate. This is
the model that transcribed the corpus.

\subsection{Correction: what the accept rule keeps}
\label{sec:e2}

Across the whole corpus, \corrReachLinesWithSpan{} of \corrReachCorrectable{}
lines (\corrReachLineShare{}) contain at least one doubtful word, with
\corrReachSpans{} marked groups in all, on \corrReachPagesWithSpan{} of
\corrReachPages{} pages (\corrReachPageShare{}). This count needs no language
model: it comes straight from the recogniser's confidences. The test sample is
\corrOffered{} of these lines, from \corrPages{} pages drawn with a fixed seed,
half from each family. Without the correction pass the text is simply the
recogniser's output; no book had a single word corrected in the original run.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \input{generated/tables/e2-correction.tex}
  \caption{Correction results by family. The sample row gives counts only: the
    sample is split equally between the families but the corpus is not, so a
    rate over the whole sample would give family~A too much weight. The
    corpus-wide rate in the text is weighted by family.}
  \label{tab:e2}
\end{table}

\corrAccepted{} of the \corrOffered{} lines were kept (table~\ref{tab:e2}).
Over the whole corpus, this means an estimated \corrAcceptRate{} of lines would
be kept. Because only a sample was tested, the true figure is uncertain, but it
very likely lies between \corrAcceptLow{} and \corrAcceptHigh{}.
\corrRewritesCaught{} replies were refused because they
changed text outside the marked words. After weighting this is
\corrRewriteRate{} of lines, and the changes add up to \corrRewriteSegments{}
segments and \corrRewriteEdits{} characters. Only \corrRewritesSubstantial{} of
these replies changed more than two characters. The other
\corrProtocolFailures{} refusals were replies that could not be read as a
correction at all, and they say nothing about the rule.

Refusals are more common where the recogniser was least sure
(figure~\ref{fig:e2-confidence}). When the least confident character on the
line scored below \corrLowConfEdge{}, \corrRefusedLowConf{} of the replies were
refused, against \corrRefusedOtherConf{} on the other lines. Only
\corrLowConfLines{} lines fall in that lowest band, however, and most of their
refusals were unreadable replies rather than rewrites.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/figures/e2-confidence.pdf}
  \caption{Share of replies refused, by the confidence of the least confident
    character on the line. The number above each bar is the number of lines in
    that band.}
  \label{fig:e2-confidence}
\end{figure}

A kept line is not always a changed line. \corrAcceptedUnchanged{} of the kept
lines came back with every marked word exactly as it was, and
\corrChangedSpans{} marked groups were actually changed.

The run used four parallel workers, which the design assumed would change the
speed but not the outcome. Replaying \corrReplayLines{} lines from
\corrReplayPages{} pages with a single worker gave the same verdict for
\corrReplayAgree{} of them. This supports the assumption, though it does not
prove that the model's output is deterministic.

\subsection{Passage location}
\label{sec:e5}

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \input{generated/tables/e5-localisation.tex}
  \caption{Passage location, on the extracts whose location the editor is sure
    of, for the two batches in which the extracts were catalogued.}
  \label{tab:e5}
\end{table}

The extracts were catalogued in two batches, shown separately in
table~\ref{tab:e5}. The locator finds the right Livre for
\locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} of the first batch and \locLivreAccuracyCatalogue{}
of the second, well above the 90\% written down beforehand.

The chapter columns measure something else. The locator returns a span of
text, not a chapter: the chapter is read from the heading above the span, as
the pipeline transcribed it, by decoding its roman numeral. That works for only
\locChapterScoreableWorkbook{} of the \locPiecesWorkbook{} extracts in the
first batch. For \locChapterUnavailableWorkbook{} no number could be decoded
from the heading, and \locChapterUnreferencedWorkbook{} is catalogued to a
Livre without a chapter. Where a number could be read, it matches the editor's
for \locChapterAccuracyWorkbook{} and \locChapterAccuracyCatalogue{} of the
extracts. The disagreements look like reading errors in the headings, not
misplaced passages. Across both batches, \locChapterNumeralClose{} of the
extracts with a different chapter number have numerals that differ by at most
two letters, such as VI read as I or LXXII as LXII, and only
\locChapterOtherMiss{} differ by more.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/figures/e5-sweep.pdf}
  \caption{Precision (top) and coverage (bottom) of the locator against its
    accept threshold. Thresholds at which nothing is accepted are left out,
    since precision is undefined there.}
  \label{fig:e5-sweep}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:e5-sweep} varies the accept threshold. Precision stays close to
100\% at every threshold in both batches, so it gives no reason to prefer one
threshold over another. Coverage does: it stays above 98\% up to a threshold of
0.86 for the first batch and 0.85 for the second, then drops to 73\% and 53\% at
0.90. The threshold the pipeline uses, 0.808, sits on the flat part of the
curve, where nothing is lost.

All \ocrPages{} pages of the \ocrBooks{} books were transcribed, in \ocrHours{}
hours at \ocrSecondsPerPage{}~s per page (table~\ref{tab:e6}).
Figure~\ref{fig:e6-throughput} shows the spread by book. The two families are
close, at \ocrSecondsPerPageFamilyA{}~s and \ocrSecondsPerPageFamilyB{}~s per
page.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/figures/e6-throughput.pdf}
  \caption{Seconds per page, by book.}
  \label{fig:e6-throughput}
\end{figure}

The pipeline also detects where chapters begin. This can only be checked on
family~A, because books~13 to~24 have no printed table of contents. On
family~A it finds \structMatchedA{} of the \structChaptersA{} chapters in the
tables of contents (\structRecallHandCheckedA{}), misses \structMissingA{}, and
proposes \structSpuriousA{} chapter openings the tables do not list, a
precision of \structPrecisionHandCheckedA{}. These rates include a reader's
hand corrections: each book's corrections file holds chapter decisions made by
hand before the check ran. They describe the corrected detector, not the
detector on its own.
