\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

The pipeline reads a volume of scans and emits structured text: per page a set
of lines, each with a role, a reading order and per-character confidences; per
volume a chapter structure calibrated against the printed table of contents
where one exists. Two optional LLM passes sit behind the recogniser. What
follows describes each stage only as far as its result is uninterpretable
without it.

\subsection{Recognition}

kraken~7.0.2~\cite{kiessling2025kraken} with a model fine-tuned from
CATMuS-Print~[Large]~\cite{gabay2024catmusprintlarge} on \trainPages{}
Transkribus pages of \emph{Trésor des Amadis}~T.1. The shipped artefact is
\file{amadis-ft.mlmodel}, identified by SHA-256 in \file{PROVENANCE.md} and
tied by weight comparison to the training checkpoint that produced it.

Two fine-tuning configurations were run. The shipped one uses a reduce-on-
plateau schedule at learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$ with augmentation; an
earlier constant-rate run at $10^{-3}$ was not shipped. Checkpoint selection
used the held-out \trainPagesVal{} pages, which is why
section~\ref{sec:limitations} calls them validation and never test.

\subsection{Layout}

Roles are assigned geometrically: baseline bands give reading order, oversize
or italic-slanted lines become titles, and lines in the top and bottom margins
become headers and footers. Illustration regions and library stamps are
suppressed rather than read. The stage is deterministic and carries no learned
component.

What it cannot settle is the ambiguous case: a single line at the top or foot
of a page that may be a running head, a catchword, or the first or last line of
the body. That residue is what experiment~E3 would score.

\subsection{The two LLM passes}

Both are opt-in, both run after recognition rather than beside it, and both
degrade to \emph{change nothing} when the model is absent or fails.

The \textbf{coherence pass} resolves an ambiguous head or foot line by asking
whether removing it lets the neighbouring lines read continuously.

The \textbf{low-confidence pass} is the one claim~C2 is about. Every word
containing a character scored below \texttt{LOWCONF\_THRESHOLD} is wrapped in
\lmark~\rmark{}, and the line is sent with its two neighbours for context. The
prompt forbids modernising the spelling. The reply is then split on the markers
and accepted only if two conditions hold: the marker count matches, and every
segment \emph{outside} the markers is byte identical to the original.
Otherwise the entire line reverts --- there is no partial acceptance.

That rule is the substance of the contribution: it makes a rewrite structurally
detectable instead of plausible, so it can be refused. Section~\ref{sec:e2}
reports how often it was, and section~\ref{sec:errors} a case it cannot
detect.

\subsection{Localisation}

Locating a \emph{Trésor} piece in the baseline text is a retrieval problem over
the full transcription of all \ocrBooks{} books. A period-aware normaliser
folds the orthographic variation that would otherwise defeat exact matching.
A rare-word seed index --- words of at least \texttt{MIN\_SEED\_LENGTH}
characters with document frequency at most \texttt{MAX\_SEED\_DF} --- proposes
candidate positions, and per-book anchor chaining assembles them into a span.
A candidate is accepted above \texttt{MIN\_SCORE}.

\subsection{Orchestration}

A batch runner drives the volumes; recognition runs on GPU and the LLM passes
run afterwards on the same card, never concurrently. The measured reason is
capacity: eight recognition workers sit near 5~GB and the correction model is
13~GB, which does not fit together on a 16~GB card.
