\begin{abstract}
% Written from the measured figures only. Every number here also appears in
% section 5; nothing is introduced by the abstract. Short sentences on purpose:
% an abstract that needs unpicking is a worse abstract.
This report evaluates a pipeline built for two related works of 16th-century
French print: \emph{Amadis de Gaule}, a \ocrBooks{}-book romance cycle, and the
\emph{Trésor des Amadis}, anthologies that reprint extracts from it. The goal is a transcription with the lowest error
rate possible. The pipeline has three stages: a kraken recognition model
fine-tuned for this print, a correction pass that asks a language model to
re-read doubtful words, and a passage locator that finds \emph{Trésor} extracts
in the transcribed text.

The recognition model was fine-tuned from CATMuS-Print on \trainPages{} pages of
the \emph{Trésor des Amadis}. Over training, its character error rate on the
validation pages fell from \trainCerFirstEpoch{} to \trainCer{}.

The correction pass marks groups of low-confidence words and asks a local
language model to correct them. A correction is kept only if the rest of the
line comes back unchanged. Tested on \corrOffered{} lines from a
\corrPages{}-page sample, it keeps an estimated \corrAcceptRate{} of
corrections over the whole corpus, and likely between \corrAcceptLow{} and
\corrAcceptHigh{}. It refused \corrRewritesCaught{} corrections that changed
text outside the marked words.

The passage locator puts \locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} of extracts in the right
Livre. All \ocrPages{} pages were
transcribed, at \ocrSecondsPerPage{}~s per page.
The scoring code, run records and result CSVs are released, and every figure
rebuilds from them without a GPU or a network. They are at
\url{https://github.com/Axl-Lvy/amadis-htr}.
