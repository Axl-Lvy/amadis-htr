\begin{abstract}
% Written from the measured figures only. Every number here also appears in
% section 5; nothing is introduced by the abstract.
A production pipeline has been transcribing a \ocrBooks{}-book cycle of
16th-century French romance for a year on the strength of one validation error
rate, measured on a 10\% page-level split of a single volume. This report
measures what that number cannot support.

Two of the three claims are tested here; recognition itself is not, because it
needs a 20-page annotated gold set that does not exist, and this report names
the absence rather than substituting a figure it can compute.

The correction pass's marker-anchor accept rule --- suspect spans wrapped in
\lmark~\rmark{}, a reply accepted only when every byte outside the markers
returns unchanged --- was run over \corrOffered{} low-confidence lines on a
seeded \corrPages{}-page sample, under a design registered beforehand. Weighted
to the corpus it accepted \corrAcceptRate{} (95\%~CI \corrAcceptLow{} to
\corrAcceptHigh{}) and refused \corrRewritesCaught{} replies for changing text
outside the markers. Measuring the rule also exposed a hole in it:
\corrFindFallbackShare{} of the offered lines took a span-locating fallback
that can mark one word twice, and on those an acceptance is not evidence that
the rule held, so the claim rests on under a third of its own evidence.

Passage localisation reaches \locLivreAccuracyWorkbook{} Livre accuracy on the
pre-registered cohort against a gate written before the numbers existed, and
\locChapterAccuracyWorkbook{} chapter accuracy. The batch runner transcribed
\ocrPages{} pages with \ocrPagesFailed{} failures at \ocrSecondsPerPage{}~s per
page.

The scoring code, the frozen run records and every result CSV are released, so
each figure rebuilds from committed inputs with no GPU, no model and no
network.
\end{abstract}
